Discrete H-bridge motor driver PCB

A four-transistor bidirectional DC motor driver, laid out from scratch in Altium Designer rather than assembled from an off-the-shelf driver module.

Altium DesignerPCB layoutBJT switchingflyback diodesDPDT switchschematic capture

The approach

An H-bridge reverses a DC motor by switching which pair of its four legs conducts. Dropping in an integrated driver IC would have made the circuit work, but it would also have hidden every decision worth learning from. Building the bridge from four discrete transistors meant confronting base drive, current paths and the consequences of layout directly.

Schematic

Each transistor is driven through a base current-limiting resistor sized for the required collector current, and flyback diodes across the motor terminals clamp the inductive kick produced when the winding is switched off — without them the transistors see a reverse spike well beyond their rating. A DPDT switch selects direction, and screw terminals bring the supply and motor connections onto the board.

Board layout

The parts of the layout that took the most attention were the ones carrying motor current: those traces have to be wide enough for the load, kept short, and arranged so the switching loop encloses as little area as possible. Component placement followed the current path rather than convenience, and the control and power sections were kept apart on the board.

What I took from it

Power electronics is where a schematic stops being an abstraction. Two circuits with identical netlists can behave very differently depending on how the board is laid out, and the only way to internalise that is to route one yourself.

Context

Electronics II coursework project.
