Differential drive robot with sensor fusion

A two-wheel differential drive platform that estimates its own position and heading by fusing two independent sources, wheel encoder odometry and an inertial gyroscope, through a Kalman filter, and reports its state over MQTT.

The idea

This is a testbed for state estimation and sensor fusion rather than a robot for its own sake.

Wheel odometry is accurate over short distances but accumulates error: every slip is integrated and never recovered. A gyroscope does not slip, but it drifts. The two failure modes are independent, which is exactly the condition under which fusion helps. A Kalman filter combines them into an estimate better than either alone, and demonstrating that improvement quantitatively is the scientific core of the project, not a side note.

Platform

A two-level chassis carrying two TT gearmotors with 57.5 mm wheels and a ball caster, driven through an L298N. Encoder discs with 20 slots and LM393 modules give roughly 9 mm of travel per pulse. An MPU6050 sits between the two wheels on the rotation axis. Power comes from two 18650 cells in series through a BMS and an LM2596 buck to the 5 V logic rail.

Wheel speed is measured from the interval between pulses rather than by counting pulses in a fixed window, at low speeds the period method stays accurate where pulse counting quantises badly.

Estimation

Differential odometry integrates the two wheel speeds into pose. The fusion layer is a one-dimensional Kalman filter on heading: prediction from the gyroscope rate with a growing covariance, and update from the odometry heading as the observation, with the measurement covariance tied to speed, at higher speed, where slip is more likely, odometry is trusted less.

The filtered heading then feeds back into the position equations. Tuning the process and measurement covariances is empirical, and recording how they were tuned is part of the result rather than an implementation detail.

Evaluation

The point of the project is the comparison, so the experiments are designed to produce numbers:

Straight line

3 m, five repetitions, lateral and longitudinal error, with and without closed-loop speed control

Rotation

360° in place, five repetitions, heading error for odometry alone, gyro alone, and fusion

Square test

1×1 m square returning to origin, both directions, the classic measure of odometry quality

Slip disturbance

Controlled slip on a low-friction surface, showing fusion degrading more gracefully than odometry alone

Environment and telemetry

An MQ-5 sensor detects combustible gas and an HC-SR04 handles obstacle avoidance. Pose, gas concentration, environmental data and battery state are published over MQTT to a broker on a laptop, with an OLED showing local state.

Status. In build. Currently working through component testing and mechanical assembly. Comparative results for odometry alone against odometry fused with the IMU will be published here, along with the firmware.
