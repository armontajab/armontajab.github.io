In progress Senior Design Project · Autumn 2026 – Spring 2027

Digital signal processing for energy consumption monitoring

A monitoring system that measures the voltage and current of mains-powered appliances, samples them digitally, transmits them over Wi-Fi, and applies digital signal processing on a central node to extract power, energy and consumption behaviour — then compares the result against published reference patterns.

The problem

A single energy figure says very little about how an appliance actually behaves. Consumption depends on the load type, operating conditions and duration, and it changes over time. Understanding that behaviour requires looking at how the electrical quantities vary, not just what they total.

There is a second gap: measuring consumption does not tell you whether it is reasonable. That judgement needs a reference to compare against. This project uses published consumption data from Tavanir and the Iranian National Standards Organisation as that baseline.

Architecture

Each monitoring unit measures the voltage and current of a load, samples both through the ESP32's ADC, and transmits the digital samples over the existing Wi-Fi network. A laptop acts as the central node: it receives, separates and organises the data from every unit, then runs the processing chain.

There is deliberately no central microcontroller. One ESP32 per monitoring unit is enough, and moving the analysis onto a laptop means the signal processing is not constrained by what fits on an embedded target.

Measurement and safety

Voltage is measured with a ZMPT101B and current with an ACS712. Because the unit connects to 220 V mains, the design separates the mains section, the measurement section and the low-voltage electronics: an insulated ABS enclosure, an RCBO for overcurrent, short-circuit and residual-current protection, a manual isolating switch, standard terminals, cable glands, and no accessible live parts.

Signal processing

The raw samples are checked for quality, filtered to remove unwanted components, and normalised before analysis. From the processed signals the system extracts RMS voltage and current, mean and standard deviation, amplitude, current variation and waveform characteristics, with frequency-domain features where they add information.

Power is then computed over defined time windows so that variation within an operating cycle is visible, and energy follows from power and time.

Analysis

Measured energy is compared against the reference pattern, and the deviation is analysed: how consumption trends over time, how power varies, how different appliances compare to each other and to their reference values, and how the load type shapes the consumption pattern. A lamp, a kettle and a vacuum cleaner were chosen as test loads precisely because they behave very differently.

Why this project

The problem itself belongs to power engineering — load behaviour, voltage, current, power, energy, consumption patterns. The method is entirely digital: sampling, processing, feature extraction and analysis. That split is the point of the project.

Status

Proposal approved. Currently in the design and construction phase, building the first monitoring unit. Results, measurements and code will be published here as the work progresses.
