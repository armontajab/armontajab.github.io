Digital Systems I, spring 2025

8-bit arithmetic datapath in 74-series logic

An 8-bit arithmetic datapath built entirely from discrete 74LS TTL devices, integrating arithmetic, data selection, decoding and storage into a single clocked path.

Proteus74LS28374LS8674LS15774LS13974LS194carry look-aheadsequential design

The idea

The goal was to assemble a small but complete datapath from standard TTL parts: something that takes two 8-bit operands, performs arithmetic and logic on them, chooses what to expose at the output, routes that output to a destination, and finally stores and shifts it. Each stage uses a different family of chip, so the finished circuit is effectively a tour of combinational and sequential building blocks.

Arithmetic

Two 74LS283 four-bit carry-look-ahead adders are cascaded into a single 8-bit adder, with the carry-out of the low stage feeding the carry-in of the high stage. Look-ahead rather than ripple carry means the carry for each bit is computed from generate and propagate terms instead of waiting for the previous stage to settle. 74LS86 XOR gates operate bitwise on the operands for comparison, high wherever two bits differ, and for parity generation.

Selection and routing

74LS157 quad 2:1 multiplexers decide what the datapath exposes: either a raw operand or the sum, with a single select line flipping the whole word between the two sources. A 74LS139 dual 2-to-4 decoder then takes a two-bit address and pulls exactly one of four output lines low, enabling the channel that receives the result.

Storage

Two 74LS194 bidirectional universal shift registers hold the 8-bit result. Their S0 and S1 control inputs select parallel load, shift left, shift right or hold, so a value can be loaded in one clock and then walked left or right on subsequent pulses.

Verification

The complete circuit was built and simulated in Proteus with logic toggles on the operands and control lines and logic probes on the outputs, so every mode could be exercised and checked against its expected truth table.

What I took from it

Working at the chip level makes the cost of every design decision visible. Choosing carry look-ahead over ripple carry is an abstract tradeoff in a textbook; here it is a specific part number and a set of wires between two packages.
