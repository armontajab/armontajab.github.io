Water level and temperature control system

Bare-metal firmware for an STM32F103C8T6 that regulates both the water level of a 50 cm tank and the water temperature, without using any third-party sensor libraries.

STM32F103C8T6ARM Cortex-M3bare-metal CTIM2 input captureEXTIADCPWMHC-SR04LM35RTCUART

The problem

The brief called for a controller holding two physical quantities at once: the height of the water in a tank and the temperature of that water. Both loops had to run continuously while a user changed setpoints from a keypad, and neither could stall while the display and clock kept updating.

Measuring the tank without a driver

An HC-SR04 ultrasonic sensor above the tank measures the distance down to the water surface, which gives the level by subtraction. The requirement was to read it without a library, so the echo pulse is timed directly using TIM2 input capture together with interrupt handling. Capturing the rising and falling edges in hardware rather than polling in software keeps the measurement accurate while the rest of the system continues to run.

Two control loops

The level loop drives the inlet pump over a 100 Hz PWM channel, scaling duty cycle with the level error: full power at maximum error, and the pump cut entirely at zero or negative error. The temperature loop is structurally the same — an LM35 is sampled through the ADC and a heating element is driven by PWM toward a 60 °C setpoint.

Interface

A 16×4 LCD shows the current level, the active setpoint, the water temperature and the RTC time on the bottom line of every screen. A 4×4 keypad and EXTI-driven navigation buttons open a menu for entering a new setpoint or setting the clock. Entered values are range-validated and rejected on screen if they fall outside the allowed band; successful changes are confirmed over UART; and if the temperature drops below its threshold the display clears and blinks a warning.

What I took from it

The real difficulty was not any single feature but the scheduling. Interrupt-driven sensing, two PWM outputs, a scanned keypad, a character display and a real-time clock all share one Cortex-M3 without any of them blocking the others.

Context

Digital Systems II final project · Supervisor: Dr. Asharioun · Team of 3 — led the design and implemented all firmware.
